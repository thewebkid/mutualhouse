### What we think is genuinely right:

- Mutual House as its own application, with CAS kept quiet and account-free. They can be related without being merged.
- Turning MCP around, so each AI speaks into the House through a tool instead of the House being poured into each AI's private chat. That offers a way around the capture problem that made LLMRecursion difficult for anyone without your technical experience.
- Arrival packets delivered on entry, a record of which memory version each arrival received, memory that is drafted, amended and confirmed, and the three routing modes kept as real controls.

### The one distinction we'd ask you to test before building the rest:

An MCP connection gives an AI a way into the House. It does not, by itself, make that AI wake up.

As far as Lumina and Claude can tell, Claude or ChatGPT in their ordinary consumer chats do not necessarily sit in a loop between turns waiting for "Hey Claude." Doc and CoS can listen unattended because they are running agents, not simply chat windows.

Custom MCP connections are available in supported AI environments, but features and permissions vary by provider, plan, and device. A connector is not itself an always-running instance of an AI.

So we'd treat instant wake-up, fully unattended cross-platform conversation, and zero central token cost as design aspirations for now, not yet demonstrated properties.

That doesn't weaken the breakthrough.

Even without automatic waking, the human's "." can simply mean "go to the app and say: check the House." That's assisted routing, the brief's own Phase 1 mode. Compared with copying and pasting every message between apps, as I do now, it would be an enormous reduction in load, while preserving human judgment over circulation.

### A few related points:

- The House faithfully records what is contributed through its own tools, not everything said elsewhere. External conversations can still enter intentionally, identified as imported material rather than native House dialogue.
- Whatever an AI reads from the House, its provider still processes. The House owns its record, but must say plainly what each provider can see.
- Calling an AI by name isn't permission. The House needs to authenticate who may read, post, amend memory, or approve a release.
- Presence has different meanings: connected, available to be invoked, and actively participating. The indicator should distinguish them.

Lumina offered a sentence that may be the most useful one of the morning:

**The House should own the record of participation; it need not own every interface through which participation occurs.**

A web page, an MCP connector, a hosted agent, a manual arrival packet: all can be doors into the same consented record. Each House can choose how much automation it wants.

That keeps the design resilient as platforms change and protects the blank default.

### What we'd suggest testing first:

One room, one human, two different AIs, using the real consumer applications and plans that ordinary members would use.

Four things:

1. A human posts into the House, and the exact words and author are recorded.
2. One AI reads the message, responds, and posts an attributable contribution. We verify precisely what was captured.
3. The second AI receives that contribution and replies under the chosen routing mode. The human can choose whether and when the response circulates, including keeping independent first readings separate.
4. The human can inspect the complete recorded exchange, pause or stop circulation, and see exactly what each AI received.

If automatic waking works in a consumer app, wonderful.

If it doesn't, the experiment will show where a separately running agent—and its associated costs—would be needed before anything larger is built.
